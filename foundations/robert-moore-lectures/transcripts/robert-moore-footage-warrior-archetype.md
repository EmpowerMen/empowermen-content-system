# The Warrior Within

## Lecture Context

This is a talk by Dr. Robert Moore on the Warrior archetype, filmed with a co-presenter (referred to as "Michael") who joins in the discussion. Moore opens with the difference between a soldier and an initiated warrior, then lays out his two-axis model of the psyche (royal–magician and lover–warrior) and argues that young men are flooded with aggressive energy before they have the wisdom to use it. He distinguishes aggression from assertiveness and rage from anger, introduces three psychoanalytic "self functions" that make up the warrior sector (aversive, adversarial, efficacy), and closes with practical ways to train the warrior (assertiveness training, martial arts, flying, project management, the study of ethics).

## Concepts Introduced

### Initiated Warrior

A man who has had his warrior energy tempered and directed in service of something beyond himself. Being armed, or having killed, does not make a man an initiated warrior. Moore calls uninitiated, violent young men "monster boys."

### Aggression vs. Assertiveness

Moore insists that everyone, male and female, carries instinctual aggression. People who haven't dealt with their shadow aggression call themselves "assertive." The task is to develop the wisdom, empathy, compassion and skill to turn raw aggression into principled assertiveness and effectiveness.

### Lover–Warrior Axis

The horizontal axis of Moore's model of the self. Young males are flooded with warrior energy before they have the wisdom to use it. Young females are flooded with lover energy and are therefore more vulnerable to predators. The flooding reverses at midlife.

### Royal–Magician Axis

The vertical axis: the necessary tension between power (the royal) and the wisdom to critique power (the magician). It is the axis of appropriate sovereignty.

### The Midlife Asymmetry

At midlife, women tend to become more effective warriors while men are flooded with the lover's need for gentleness. Moore links this unacknowledged shift to the high rate of divorce at midlife.

### Aversive Self Function

The capacity to recognise what is toxic and form a secure boundary against it. Moore describes it as biological, present even in cellular organisms.

### Adversarial Self Function

The capacity to confront what should be confronted, to say no, and to advocate for a more just vision. People who avoid conflict are immature in this sector of the personality.

### Efficacy Self Function

The capacity to be effective. It is the foundation of legitimate self-esteem, as opposed to grandiose self-esteem that comes from an unearned flooding of the royal sector. Moore credits Ernest Wolf with the term.

### Rage vs. Anger

Rage is unregulated, archetypal warrior energy that wants to destroy everything and does not care about justice. Anger seeks the right and aims to correct things without destroying them. Easy rage and extreme sensitivity to shame mark a lack of warrior initiation.

### Passive Pole of the Missing Warrior

Without a warrior initiation, a man becomes passive, ineffectual and masochistic, and may seek out humiliation by others' aggression as his only connection to his own. Moore reports that this resolves as men claim their aggression in a mature, responsible way.

### The Price of Nice

Drawn from George Bach's *Creative Aggression*. "Nice" is a standard we apply to boys, not men, and worshipping it creates costs in families and society.

### Warrior as the Seat of Moral Discernment

Of the four archetypal sectors, only the warrior carries a sense of right and wrong, good and evil, and honour and shame about collusion with evil. Without it, a person can be "blissed out" and incapable of discerning real evil.

## Practical Applications

### For Personal Development

- Take an assertiveness training course, or practise one small assertive act in each area of life this week
- Study a martial art such as aikido if you tend toward pacifism or "niceness"
- Pick up a skill with real consequences and checklists, such as piloting, where cutting corners shows immediately

### For Relationships

- Notice when you go passive-aggressive instead of saying a clear no
- Practise holding a boundary calmly rather than withdrawing or exploding
- At midlife, keep up the capacities the other half of the partnership is now developing: gentleness for the warrior, clarity and action for the lover

### For Leadership

- Learn project management: ask the right questions, track tasks, find where the sloppiness is
- Study ethics and keep your outrage at real wrong intact
- Build the efficacy of the people you mentor by encouraging them to develop real skills
