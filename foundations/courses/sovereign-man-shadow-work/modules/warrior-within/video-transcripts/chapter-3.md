## CHAPTER 3 TRANSCRIPT

### Identify Areas of Your Warrior Energy for Development

Welcome to Chapter 3 of The Warrior Within module. This video accompanies Workbook Exercise 2, where we’ll identify areas of your Warrior energy for development.

Let’s begin by exploring Dr. Robert Moore’s concept of developmental lines, focusing on the Warrior quadrant. Moore describes this as representing both the capacity for aggression and the ability to serve a cause. The Warrior energy embodies focused discipline, boundaries, service, and mission. It’s the ability to get organized, motivated, and follow the vision found in the royal line of development.

Without the Warrior, there’s no motivation or energy to access for a goal. It defends the boundaries of the “I am” and the “I want.” When immature or undeveloped, this energy can cause trouble, from passivity to rampant violence. Signs of a shortage in Warrior energy include failure to defend boundaries in relationships, lack of focus, and absence of clear goals.

### Completing the Warrior Energy Wheel Exercise

In your workbook exercise, we’ve adapted Dr. Moore’s work into a wheel-of-life style assessment. You’ll score yourself in various areas based on your personal satisfaction level. We’ve also added a crucial component: fortitude.

By fortitude, we mean strength through resilience and the ability to overcome. To be committed to life as growth and overcoming is to be willing to accept suffering and risk pain. This encompasses both physical and mental strength.

---

### The Six Warrior Energy Categories

- **Fortitude:** More than physical power; embodies inner strength, courage, and resilience.
- **Aggression:** Assertiveness and the ability to take action when necessary; confronting challenges with determination.
- **Boundary Formation:** Establishing and maintaining healthy boundaries to protect personal space, values, and beliefs.
- **Focus and Discipline:** Channeling energy towards specific goals; fostering purpose and direction.
- **Protection:** Willingness to defend against threats, ensuring safety and wellbeing of self and others.
- **Service:** Using strength and skills to support and assist others professionally or through acts of kindness.
