## CHAPTER 4 TRANSCRIPT

### Facing Your King Shadow

Welcome to Chapter 4 of The King Within module. This video accompanies Workbook Exercise 3, where we’ll face your King shadow and work towards integrating it.

This exercise invites you to confront and integrate the challenging facets of the King archetype, paving the way for more balanced and effective personal sovereignty.

The term “king shadow” refers to the “dark side” of the king archetype within the masculine psyche — the aspects of ourselves that we’ve split off, rejected, denied, hidden from ourselves, projected onto others, or otherwise disowned.

For example, Thomas, who has no children, has just been told by his girlfriend that she is pregnant. Thomas immediately demands she gets an abortion. Here his king shadow is revealed, as he acts on an impulse of avoiding responsibility (Weakling King) or self-preservation (Tyrant King), both signs of an immature male. Now, there may be many reasons why Thomas reacted in this way, but it’s clear shadow work is needed, which could reveal he knows this isn’t the right relationship for him, or that he is afraid of becoming a father. Either scenario indicates a suppression of his energy, which has manifested in a shadow form of the king.

It takes a lot of energy to hide the undesirable aspects of our king energy from ourselves. The goal of king shadow work is to free up that energy, which we can then use for growth and transformation.

### Signs Your King Energy May Be in Shadow

The Shadow King can switch between inflated and deflated states.

When over-functioning, you may exhibit heightened sensitivity to criticism, often leading to feelings of weakness and deflation even in response to subtle remarks. You may struggle to express your feelings and reject support when offered, driven by a fear of failure that prompts you to take on excessive burdens and attempt to solve everything at once. It is crucial for you to acknowledge these feelings and work on strengthening your ability to handle triggers that evoke such responses.

**Inflated Shadow Aspects (The Tyrant) to watch for in yourself include:**

- Arrogance
- Neglect
- Self-centeredness
- Terrified of own weakness
- “Because I say so” attitude
- “My way or the highway” approach
- Thriving on power through shaming or threatening
- Grandiosity and defensiveness
- “Don’t criticize me!” stance

**Deflated Shadow Aspects (The Weakling) to watch for in yourself include:**

- Abdication of responsibility to self and others
- Hiding and indecision
- Weakness and lack of purpose
- Avoidance of conflict
- “It’s not my fault!” mentality
- Taking no responsibility
- “Whatever you say…” attitude
- Unsure of self, weak, avoids conflict
- Over-dependence
- Feeding the Tyrant by giving up power

### Instructions for Workbook Exercise

In your workbook, take your time to answer the shadow work prompts in your own time. Complete a section and then take a break before attempting another one to avoid feeling overwhelmed and to allow yourself to properly process the material you have brought into consciousness.

You may not be able to write or think of anything for some sections; this is fine and to be expected. Simply move onto the next one or take a break. If something comes to mind later on, return to the exercise to complete it.

These shadow work prompts essentially ask you the question:  
**“What did you come to believe about yourself when that thing happened?”** Only you can answer that truthfully.

Find a quiet space and some time. Switch off all distractions and begin the exercise when you are ready.
