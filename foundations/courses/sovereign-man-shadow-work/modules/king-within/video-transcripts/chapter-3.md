### Identify Areas of Your King Energy for Development

Welcome to Chapter 3 of the King Within module. This video accompanies Workbook Exercise 2, where we’ll identify areas of your King energy for development.

We begin by exploring Dr. Robert Moore’s concept of developmental lines, focusing on the King quadrant. Moore describes this as an instinctual line related to nurturing and centering within the human personality. The King archetype is crucial for developing a sense of self, vision, and desire.

To gauge your King energy, ask yourself:  
*“When did I last genuinely bless and affirm another person?”*  
Was it today, yesterday, or longer ago? Do you do this spontaneously, or is it an effort? If it’s infrequent or challenging, you may be short on King energy — a common situation for many men.

In your workbook exercise, we’ve adapted Dr. Moore’s work into a wheel-of-life style assessment. You’ll score yourself in various areas based on your personal satisfaction level. We’ve also added a crucial component: responsibility.

As Rod Boothroyd explains in his book *Warrior Magician Lover King*, responsibility is key to the King archetype. The King is the one who has the sign over his desk saying *“The Buck Stops Here.”* If the kingdom isn’t functioning well, if the children aren’t flourishing, if the employees are rebelling, if the business isn’t prospering, whose responsibility is it, other than the King’s?

This exercise will help you identify your strengths and areas for improvement within your King energy. By understanding where you excel and where you can grow, you’ll be better equipped to develop a more balanced and mature masculine presence.

Remember, this self-assessment is a powerful tool for personal transformation. As you work through the exercise, be honest with yourself. Recognizing areas for growth is not a weakness — it’s the first step towards becoming the king of your own life.

### Completing the King Energy Wheel Exercise

This exercise will help you assess your current King energy across six crucial categories.

For each of the six categories, draw a line across the segment representing your satisfaction score. Use a scale from 1 (very dissatisfied) to 10 (fully satisfied). Trust your gut feeling rather than your assumptions.

Be honest with yourself, as this will provide the most valuable insights for your journey towards embodying mature King energy.

A helpful tip: Pretend the score 7 isn’t available. This will push you to decide if you’re truly satisfied or dissatisfied in each area. Scores above 7 indicate satisfaction; below 7 indicate potential areas for personal work.

---

### The Six Categories Explained

- **Responsibility:** Taking ownership of your actions and decisions; being answerable and reliable for yourself and others.
- **Power:** Your ability to lead, make decisions, and influence others.
- **Ordering and Provision:** Creating structure and ensuring needs are met; establishing rules, systems, and providing for your community or family.
- **Centering and Calmness:** Your inner stability; remaining composed and grounded in challenging situations; bringing peace to those around you.
- **Inclusive Caring:** Care for family, friends, and community; fostering an environment prioritizing individual wellbeing and growth.
- **Blessing:** Using your authority to empower others; recognizing and nurturing the unique gifts and efforts of those around you.

### Identifying Strengths and Gaps

Now that you have completed the King Energy Wheel, reflect on your daily life. For each category, identify activities, habits, or behaviors that align with the King archetype. This will help you recognize how you already embody King energy and where you might need to develop further.

List what you already do in your life that fits within each category. Some activities may apply to multiple areas. Some sections may be fuller than others — that’s fine.

If you have nothing to add to a category, simply move on.

Examples:

- **Responsibility:** Being a parent, managing family duties.
- **Power:** Leading a team at work, making important decisions.
- **Ordering and Provision:** Managing household finances, organizing schedules.
- **Centering and Calmness:** Daily meditation, stress management practices.
- **Inclusive Caring:** Volunteering in community groups, supporting friends.
- **Blessing:** Mentoring colleagues, encouraging others’ growth.

Review your entries and notice any imbalances. These likely reflect your satisfaction levels in the wheel scores. Areas with fewer entries may indicate opportunities for growth.
